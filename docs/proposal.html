Sweep: one trip, every errand

Real-world problem

A weekly errand list usually spans several kinds of shop: supermarket, chemist, stationer, hardware store. People either go to the nearest shop again and again, overpaying and backtracking, or chase the lowest price per item and zig-zag across the suburb. Neither accounts for opening hours or stock. Sweep turns the list into one optimised trip: which shops, what to buy where, and in what order.

Target user

Students and residents of Powai, Mumbai, running errands on foot, by cycle or by auto-rickshaw, who want to spend less money and time.

Proposed features

  • Autocomplete list builder with quantities, example lists, departure time and travel mode
  • Bloom-filter pre-screen of every open shop's stock, confirmed exactly against inventory
  • Shop selection by weighted set cover, with a price-vs-distance slider
  • Route ordering with a 2-approximate metric-TSP tour plus 2-opt, shown on an animated map
  • Per-item deal score against the city median; comparison with two common shopping strategies
  • "X-ray" view of each algorithm's trace and a "Lab" view of live server-side benchmarks
  • Clear handling of empty lists, unknown or duplicate items, closed shops and insufficient stock

Core techniques and why they fit

TechniqueUnit / COWhy it is appropriate
Greedy weighted set cover + exact DP baselineU5 · CO4/CO6"Fewest, cheapest shops that together stock the list" is exactly set cover (NP-hard). The greedy gives an H(n) guarantee in polynomial time.
Metric TSP 2-approximation (Kruskal MST with union-find, preorder shortcut, 2-opt) + Held-Karp baselineU2, U5 · CO2/CO4Ordering the stops is TSP on straight-line distances, which form a metric, so the double-tree bound of 2·OPT applies.
Bloom filter with random saltU6 · CO5Shop inventories are mostly "no". A few-hundred-bit sketch per shop rejects about 85% of shop–item checks with no false negatives, and every "maybe" is confirmed exactly.
Randomized quickselect / quicksortU6 · CO5Median and extreme prices per item in expected O(n), safe on sorted or duplicate-heavy price lists. Also orders Kruskal's edges.
Trie over word-start suffixesU4 · CO3Prefix autocomplete that matches any word of a name, at a cost independent of catalogue size.

Data source

Realistic synthetic dataset from a seeded generator committed to the repo: 36 shops of 9 types around real Powai localities, with opening hours; 176 items in 12 categories with MRP-style prices; 770 inventory listings with price and stock. Distances are straight-line × 1.3 road factor, which keeps the triangle inequality.

Measurable success criteria

  • 100% of returned picks are stocked, with enough units, at the assigned shop. Zero Bloom false negatives, and observed FP rate within sampling error of theory.
  • Set-cover cost ≤ H(n)·OPT (λ = 0), and route ≤ 2·OPT, both measured against exact solvers. Target average route gap below 5%.
  • Trip cost no worse than "nearest store first" and "cheapest everywhere" on sample lists.
  • At least 5 JUnit tests per algorithm including edge cases; benchmarks at three or more input sizes; a plan in under 50 ms.

Stack

Java 21 + Spring Boot REST (all algorithms self-implemented), JUnit 5, plain HTML/CSS/JS frontend served by the same app.
